Build it in four steps

Start on everyday objects before trying outdoor recordings.

◉〰
1. Start with a wired disc

Pre-attached leads make the first build easier and reduce the risk of damaging the sensor.

🔌
2. Connect and insulate

Follow the sensor and input instructions. Insulate each connection separately so wires cannot touch.

🌱◉
3. Attach gently

Use a soft band or removable putty on a fallen leaf, dry twig, cardboard, or wooden surface.

🎧〰
4. Listen at low gain

Tap lightly and raise input gain gradually to avoid loud pops or clipping.

04

Real-world listening challenges

Record these examples yourself. The results depend on the surface and setup; do not label them insect calls without evidence.

🍃　〰
Fallen leaf or dry stem

Try a gentle tap and light rubbing. Compare how a dry leaf and firm twig carry vibrations.

🪵　◉
Wood, cardboard, paper cup

Tap each item with the same fingertip. Compare resonance, pitch, and handling noise.

🌰　〰
Dry seed pod or bark

Try a very light touch on a fallen pod or loose bark. Avoid damaging living plants or disturbing wildlife.

Real build references: Zach Poff's durable contact-mic guide and Make: Magazine's piezo contact-mic project show actual hardware and wiring approaches.

Care and safety: Keep electronics dry, insulate exposed conductors, and never connect bare wires to an unknown port or speaker output. Do not attach sensors to insects or animals, pierce living plants, or tightly clamp stems. A contact mic detects structure-borne vibration; it does not identify a species.

Curious minds ask…

Can insects really make sounds?

Yes. Depending on species, sounds can be made by rubbing body parts together, wing movement, drumming, or other mechanisms. Many arthropods also sense vibrations rather than airborne sound.

Why use real recordings?

Real recordings preserve the texture and variation of animal behavior. Synthetic tones test the exhibit but should never be presented as a species' actual call.

Can we add live insect sensors later?

Yes. A future version could connect a contact microphone, piezo sensor, or ultrasonic detector to a small computer and stream authorized sensor output to this page.
